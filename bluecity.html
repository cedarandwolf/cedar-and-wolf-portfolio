Our Story

Where gothic
tradition lives

Blue City was built in 1978 as a refuge for those who wanted tattooing treated as a fine art — no flash walls, no walk-ins, no shortcuts.

The Beginning

A studio built
on obsession

Blue City was founded by Vincent Morel, a French-born engraver who arrived in Los Angeles in 1977 with a suitcase, a set of etching needles, and a vision for what tattooing could be.

He set up a small appointment-only studio on Cahuenga Boulevard, refusing to produce anything he couldn't call art. The waiting list was eight months long within a year.

The Philosophy

Craft
above all else

We believe that permanent marks deserve permanent thought. Every client at Blue City goes through a consultation process designed to understand not just what they want — but why.

Our artists specialize in the intersection of gothic tradition, fine art illustration, and the physical language of tattooing. We don't rush. We don't compromise. We build work that lasts a lifetime.

History

Four decades of ink

  • 1978
    Founded on Cahuenga

    Vincent Morel opens Blue City as a one-artist, appointment-only studio. Specializes in engraving-style blackwork inspired by 19th-century European illustration.

  • 1986
    Collective expands

    Three additional artists join the studio. Blue City moves to a larger space on Hollywood Boulevard. Fine line and gothic lettering added as specialties.

  • 1998
    Second generation

    Morel's protégé Marcus Vane becomes lead artist. The studio doubles in size. Blue City earns its first feature in Inked Magazine.

  • 2012
    Current home

    Blue City moves to its current location — a converted Victorian building in Hollywood with dedicated consultation rooms, a private gallery, and a street-facing flash window.

  • Now
    Eight artists, one vision

    Eight resident artists, 12,000+ pieces, and the same commitment to craft that Vincent carried in from Paris. Still appointment-only. Still obsessed.

Our Principles

How we work

Consultation First

Every project begins with a face-to-face or video consultation. We don't touch skin until we fully understand your intent.

Custom Every Time

We don't repeat designs. Every piece is drawn from scratch for the person wearing it. Flash is for reference, not for tracing.

Lifetime Quality

We use only professional-grade inks and equipment. Our technique is designed to age well — not just look good on day one.

Healing Guide

Aftercare that
protects your work

The ink we apply is only the first half of the work. How you care for it in the weeks after determines how it will look for the rest of your life.

  • 01
    Keep it wrapped
    Leave the initial wrap on for 2–4 hours, or overnight if using second-skin film. Don't peel early.
  • 02
    Wash gently
    Use lukewarm water and unscented soap. Pat dry with a clean paper towel — never a cloth towel.
  • 03
    Moisturize lightly
    Apply a thin layer of unscented lotion 2–3 times daily. Don't over-saturate — thin layers heal faster.
  • 04
    Avoid sun & water
    No swimming, soaking, or direct sun for 3 weeks. After healing, always use SPF 50+ on tattooed skin.
  • 05
    Let it peel
    The skin will flake and peel in week 2. Don't pick or scratch — let it shed naturally to preserve detail.

How It Works

The Blue City process

01
Inquiry

Submit your idea through our booking form. Tell us the style, size, placement, and any reference imagery you have in mind.

02
Consultation

Meet in person or via video with your matched artist. We discuss your concept, refine the direction, and agree on a design approach.

03
Design

Your artist creates a custom drawing. You see the design before your appointment date — revisions are included.

04
Session

Arrive, approve the stencil placement, and let the artist work. Aftercare packs and healing guides provided at every appointment.

Blackwork · Gothic · Ornamental

Marcus is Blue City's lead artist and creative director. Trained under founder Vincent Morel, he has spent 26 years refining a style that merges European engraving tradition with the visceral weight of blackwork.

His pieces are architectural in nature — built from interior structure outward, with a recurring obsession with negative space and the tension between filled and empty.

Fine Line · Portrait · Botanical

Celeste approaches tattooing from a drawing background — she studied at CalArts before transitioning to fine line tattooing in 2012. Her work is recognizable for its restraint: every line placed with complete deliberation, nothing wasted.

She specializes in portrait work and botanical illustration, often combining the two into compositions that feel like pages from a Victorian naturalist's journal.

Neo-Traditional · Color · Cover-Up

Rein was born in Copenhagen and trained at one of Denmark's oldest tattoo shops before relocating to Los Angeles in 2016. He brings a Scandinavian discipline to neo-traditional work — technically precise, emotionally controlled.
